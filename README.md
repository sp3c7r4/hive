
 What Is Hive?

 Hive is an online teaching and learning platform built for the Nigerian market.
 Think of it as a space where instructors can build their own teaching
 communities, sell courses, and earn money — while students learn, parents keep
 an eye on their kids' progress, and administrators keep the whole ecosystem
 running smoothly.

 At its core, Hive is about community-driven learning. An instructor doesn't
 just upload a random course into a void — they create a community (like a
 mini-school or academy), and that community houses their courses. Students join
 communities that interest them, enroll in courses, and work through lessons at
 their own pace.

 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 The Four Roles

 Hive has four types of users, each with a completely different experience:

 ### 1. The Instructor

 The instructor is someone who wants to teach and earn. They might be a
 professional, a skilled practitioner, or an academic.

 What they can do:

 - Create communities. A community is their branded teaching space — like
   "Design Academy" or "Master Math." Each community has its own landing page,
   member roster, and course catalog. Communities can be public (anyone can
   join), private (invite-only), or require approval to join.

 - Build courses. Inside a community, the instructor builds courses. Each course
   is structured into modules (sections/chapters), and each module contains
   lessons. Lessons can be videos, PDFs, live classes, quizzes, or assignments.
   The instructor drags and drops to organize everything.

 - Set pricing. Courses can be free, one-time payment, or subscription-based
   (monthly). Communities themselves can also be paid — meaning students pay a
   recurring fee just to be a member.

 - Manage members. They can approve or reject join requests, assign roles
   (admin, member), block users, and invite people via email or shareable links.

 - Earn money and withdraw. Every time a student pays for a course or community
   membership, the instructor earns revenue (minus a 10% platform fee). Money
   sits in a pending balance for 7 days (a holding period), then moves to an
   available balance they can withdraw to their bank account.

 - Track their business. The instructor dashboard shows revenue summaries,
   enrollment trends, active student counts, and a feed of recent activity (new
   enrollments, reviews, payments).

 In short: The instructor is running a teaching business. Hive handles the
 infrastructure — hosting, payments, community management — so they can focus on
 teaching.

 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 ### 2. The Student

 The student is someone who wants to learn. They might be a young person picking
 up a skill, a professional upskilling, or a hobbyist.

 What they can do:

 - Explore and discover. They browse communities and courses by category,
   difficulty level, price, and rating. They can see the full curriculum outline
   before paying — lesson titles and structure are visible, but the actual
   content is locked until they enroll.

 - Join communities and enroll in courses. Depending on community settings,
   joining might be instant (free public), require payment, or require the
   instructor's approval.

 - Learn at their own pace. Once enrolled, they work through lessons in order
   (or freely, depending on how the instructor set it up). The platform tracks
   their progress with a progress bar. They can resume right where they left
   off.

 - Participate. They take quizzes, submit assignments for grading, attend live
   classes with a built-in video player, and interact in the community feed
   (posts, comments, likes).

 - Earn certificates. When they complete a course and meet the minimum
   requirements (attendance, quiz scores, completion percentage), they get a
   certificate.

 - Stay motivated. The dashboard shows their learning streak (how many
   consecutive days they've learned), upcoming live classes, and a "Continue
   Learning" section to jump back in with one tap.

 In short: The student browses, enrolls, learns, completes, and earns
 certificates — all within communities they've chosen to be part of.

 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 ### 3. The Parent

 The parent is someone who wants to monitor a child's education. They might be a
 mother, father, guardian, or sponsor paying for a student's courses.

 What they can do:

 - Link to a student. They send a link request by entering the student's email.
   The student must approve the link before the parent can see anything — this
   protects the student's privacy.

 - Monitor progress. Once linked, parents see each child's enrolled courses with
   progress bars, recent activity (lessons completed, quizzes taken, assignments
   submitted), average scores, and learning streaks.

 - Manage multiple children. A parent can link to several students (e.g.,
   multiple kids) and see each one's dashboard separately.

 - Handle payments. Parents can pay for courses on behalf of their linked
   students.

 What they cannot do:
 - They cannot take courses themselves (unless they also create a student
   account).
 - They cannot see data for students who haven't approved the link.

 In short: The parent is an overseer. They keep tabs on how their kids are doing
 without being able to interfere with the learning experience itself.

 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 ### 4. The Super Admin

 The super admin is the platform operator — someone from the Hive company
 itself. This role has full visibility and control over everything.

 What they can do:

 - Manage users. View, search, filter, and take action on all instructors,
   students, and parents across the entire platform.

 - Manage communities. See all communities, moderate content, resolve disputes.

 - Oversee payments and withdrawals. Monitor all financial transactions, approve
   or flag withdrawals, handle refunds.

 - Content moderation. Review flagged posts, comments, and courses.

 - Platform analytics. High-level dashboards showing user growth, revenue,
   engagement, and other metrics across the entire platform.

 - Activity logs. A complete audit trail of significant actions on the platform
   — who did what and when.

 In short: The super admin keeps the marketplace healthy and trustworthy.

 Key Concepts (Non-Technical)

 ### Communities vs. Courses

 A community is the container — like a school or academy. It has members, a feed
 (like a mini social network), and houses multiple courses.

 A course is the actual learning content — structured modules and lessons. It
 lives inside a community.

 A student joins a community first, then enrolls in courses within it.

 ### Payments

 All money on Hive is in Naira (₦) . When a student pays:

 1. The payment goes through a payment gateway (Paystack, Flutterwave, or
    Stripe).
 2. The platform confirms the payment.
 3. The student gets access to the course immediately.
 4. 10% goes to the platform. The instructor's 90% goes into a pending balance.
 5. After 7 days, the funds move to the instructor's available balance.
 6. The instructor can withdraw available funds to their Nigerian bank account.

 This 7-day holding period exists to handle refunds and disputes.

 ### Privacy and Linking

 Students have control over who sees their data. A parent cannot monitor a
 student without the student's explicit approval. The link can be revoked at any
 time by either party.

 ### Soft Delete

 Nothing on Hive is ever truly deleted. When something is "deleted," it's hidden
 from view but remains in the system for 30 days (to comply with Nigeria's data
 protection regulations). This means accidental deletions can be recovered.

 In a Nutshell

 Hive is a Nigerian marketplace for education. Instructors build teaching
 businesses, students find and complete courses, parents oversee their
 children's learning, and administrators keep the platform safe and functional.
 Everything revolves around communities — instructors create them, students join
 them, and learning happens inside them.
